Score two labellings of the same items against each other and the whole input is a contingency table, so the chance level is a finite sum rather than something you shuffle for — and it is not zero. Under the fixed-margin permutation null the expected mutual information has a closed form, and a labelling that knows nothing at all about the truth scores an exact 0.5637 on homogeneity at k' = 100 on 240 items. So here is the comparison that matters: a uniformly random 93-cluster labelling scores homogeneity 0.5033 while a clustering that genuinely gets half the items right scores 0.3118 — and on the same two tables the chance-corrected controls read 0.0003 against 0.2919, the right way round, and by 0.29 of the index’s range. The exact k' at which pure chance overtakes that half-right clustering is 50 for homogeneity, 92 geometric, 116 arithmetic, and never for ARI or AMI, whose chance level is exactly 0 — a fact rather than a convention. Then the version of this that ships in papers: 240 points on a line with 5 real clusters, the exactly optimal k-means solution at every k from 1 to n by dynamic programming, and k chosen by maximising the agreement score. Homogeneity, purity and both min-normalised scores all choose k = 240 — one point per cluster — completeness chooses k = 1, and V-measure, NMI, ARI and AMI choose 5. What does a clustering score mean?

0. Why this and not another algorithm

This series has built k-means, DBSCAN, a Gaussian mixture with EM, hierarchical clustering and spectral clustering, and every one of those days ended the same way: a number was printed and the number was good, so the algorithm worked. Day 72 stepped out of an identification arc to ask whether an experiment was big enough. This one asks the question underneath all five clustering days at once — what the printed number is a number of — and it turns out to be answerable in closed form, because the score does not see the data. It sees a contingency table of counts, and the space of tables with fixed margins is finite.

1. The score never touches the data

Take the truth U with r classes and a candidate clustering V with c clusters. Cross-tabulate: nij is the number of items in class i and cluster j, with margins ai and bj. Every external clustering metric in common use — mutual information and its four normalisations, homogeneity, completeness, V-measure, purity, the Rand index, adjusted Rand, Fowlkes–Mallows, Jaccard, variation of information — is a function of that table and nothing else. Two completely different datasets that produce the same table get the same score, by construction.

That is what makes the chance level computable. “What would this score be if the clustering knew nothing?” becomes a question about a finite set of integer matrices, and the natural null is: hold both margins fixed and permute the labels. The table is then multiple-hypergeometric, and expectations are sums.

2. Two families, two different defects

The pair-counting family counts pairs of items. With S = Σ C(nij,2), A = Σ C(ai,2), B = Σ C(bj,2) and P = C(n,2), the Rand index is (P − A − B + 2S)/P and

E[S] = A B / P            (exact, under fixed margins)
ARI  = (S - A B / P) / ((A+B)/2 - A B / P)

so the adjusted Rand index is the raw agreement with its chance level subtracted and its range rescaled. Given the margins the denominator is a constant, so E[ARI] = 0 exactly — not approximately, not on average over datasets, but exactly, for every pair of margins.

The information family measures how much knowing the cluster tells you about the class. MI has a chance level too, and it is not zero and not small:

E[MI] = sum_i sum_j sum_q (q/n) ln(n q / (a_i b_j)) * P_hyper(q; n, a_i, b_j)

That is Vinh, Epps and Bailey's formula, and it is what the MEASURE tab computes. The folklore shortcut (r−1)(c−1)/2n comes from the chi-square approximation to 2n·MI, and it is wrong by tens of percent in both directions — 16% low at k' = 32 and 32% high at k' = n on the default world — so the page prints both.

3. Splitting is free, and three metrics pay for it

Here is the mechanism behind the whole page in one line. If V is a refinement of U — every cluster sits inside one class — then V determines U, so H(U|V) = 0 and MI = H(U) exactly. Not nearly; exactly, and independently of how small the pieces are. Homogeneity is defined as 1 − H(U|V)/H(U), which is MI/H(U), so homogeneity is 1.000 for every refinement including the one with n clusters of one item each. Purity is 1 there too. And because H(V) ≥ H(U) for any refinement, min(H(U),H(V)) = H(U), so NMI under min normalisation is also exactly 1.

Completeness, MI/H(V), has the mirror-image defect: it is 1 for the single cluster containing everything. That is why V-measure exists — it is the harmonic mean of the two, and the harmonic mean of MI/H(U) and MI/H(V) is exactly 2MI/(H(U)+H(V)), which is NMI with arithmetic normalisation. Two metrics that appear in different papers under different names are the same function; the page asserts it at 1e-15 and measures 1.1e-16.

4. The number that made this page

Adjusting for chance is supposed to fix this, and for the pair-counting family it does. For the information family it fixes only half the problem, because the adjustment is

AMI = (MI - E[MI]) / (norm - E[MI])

and the normaliser is still in there. Take any refinement again. The numerator is H(U) − E[MI]. Under min normalisation the denominator is min(H(U),H(V)) − E[MI] = H(U) − E[MI] — the same number, term for term. So AMI is exactly 1.000000000000 for every refinement of the truth: two dozen clusters or a hundred and twenty, all of them a perfect score from a chance-corrected metric. And at the singleton limit E[MI] = H(U) as well, because with every column margin equal to 1 every relabelling gives the same table up to permutation, so numerator and denominator are both exactly zero. The machine then divides one rounding error by another and reports 0.988, or 1.007 on a balanced world, or whatever the last bit happens to be. The page prints the numerator and the denominator next to the answer so this is visible rather than argued.

5. Choosing k with the metric

The MEASURE tab does the thing benchmark tables do. Points on a line, five real clusters, and for every k from 1 to n the exactly optimal k-means partition — one-dimensional k-means has an exact dynamic program, so there are no restarts, no seeds and no local minima to blame. Then pick the k whose clustering the metric likes best.

Homogeneity picks k = n. Purity picks k = n. NMI and AMI under min normalisation pick k = n. Completeness picks k = 1. V-measure, NMI (arithmetic — scikit-learn's current default), ARI and AMI pick the true k. That is not a subtle bias to be corrected with a penalty term; it is four shipped metrics whose maximum over the whole family is the degenerate answer.

And the reason is worth having, because it says which metrics are safe rather than merely which ones passed this time. The degenerate answer's score is itself a closed form: at k' = n homogeneity, purity and both min-normalised scores are 1, geometric NMI is √(H(U)/ln n), arithmetic NMI and V-measure are 2H(U)/(H(U)+ln n), and the adjusted Rand index and AMI are exactly zero. A metric is safe from this failure only if that floor is zero, and only the last two are. Drop the separation slider to 1.5 and watch it happen: the best honest clustering can no longer beat √(H(U)/ln n) = 0.5246, and geometric NMI's maximum moves to k' = n as well. It was never immune, only comfortable.

6. The ranking inversion

An argmax on a nested family is a strong statement but a slightly artificial one, so the page also does the plainest possible comparison. Two candidates. One is a uniformly random labelling into many clusters — it knows nothing. The other has the right number of clusters and half its labels reassigned at random — it knows something. Homogeneity ranks the one that knows nothing above the one that knows something — 0.5033 against 0.3118 on the default world — while the adjusted Rand index reads 0.0003 against 0.2919 and ranks them the right way round across almost a third of its range.

The exact version of that statement is the threshold table: hold the half-right control fixed and raise k' until the chance level of the ignorant labelling overtakes it. For homogeneity it takes 50 clusters, for geometric normalisation 92, for arithmetic 116, and for ARI and AMI it never happens at any k', because their chance level is zero at every k'. Those thresholds are exact numbers rather than measurements, and they say something practical: the vulnerability is not “NMI is badly behaved”, it is “NMI is badly behaved once the number of clusters is a noticeable fraction of the number of items”, which is exactly the regime of single-cell, community detection and near-duplicate work.

7. Zero on average is not zero

ARI's chance level is exactly zero, and that is not the same as ARI being zero when nothing is going on. Enumerate every table with three balanced classes over thirty items — 2,211 of them, weighted by the hypergeometric law — and the null standard deviation of ARI is about 0.05, the 95th percentile is about 0.098 and the 99th is about 0.162. So a reported ARI of 0.12 on thirty items is inside the range that pure chance produces one time in twenty, and the honest response is not a rule of thumb about what counts as weak agreement. It is a permutation test — and because the null is enumerable, the p-value is exact and costs a few milliseconds. The page prints it along a corruption path so you can see where the score stops being consistent with chance.

8. The controls are not free either

None of this is an argument that ARI and AMI are the answer and the rest is a mistake. They disagree with each other, and the page finds where. Drop the separation to 2 and geometric NMI and geometric AMI choose k = 3 while arithmetic NMI, ARI and arithmetic AMI choose 4, and the truth is 5 — none of them right, and not even wrong in the same direction. Over the candidate board ARI and AMI invert 2 of the 55 pairs. The known shape of that disagreement is that the pair-counting family is dominated by large clusters — C(a,2) grows quadratically, so the 83-item class contributes 14.7 times the pairs of the 22-item one while being under four times its size — while the information family weights clusters by their probability. Neither is wrong. They are answering different questions, and a paper that reports one of them without saying which normalisation it used has not reported a number at all.

9. What to actually do

Three things, all cheap. Report the chance level next to the score, because it is a closed form and takes microseconds. Say which normalisation you used, because V-measure, NMI-min, NMI-geometric and NMI-arithmetic are four different numbers off one table and they do not even agree on the ordering. And if the number of clusters is not fixed in advance, do not select it with a metric whose maximum is at k = n — check that first, on your own family of solutions, with the two lines of code the MEASURE tab is running.

🔨 How it is built

1. A finite sample space, so the truth is a sum

Every score on the page is a function of a contingency table, and the permutation null fixes both margins, so the null sample space is the finite set of non-negative integer matrices with those margins. enumerateTables walks it by recursion over cells, keeping the remaining row total and the remaining column totals and pruning a branch the moment the columns to the right cannot absorb what is left. Each completed table gets the multiple hypergeometric weight ∏ai! ∏bj! / (n! ∏nij!), built out of log-gammas. Thirty items in three balanced classes is 2,211 tables and a few milliseconds; the weights are then asserted to sum to 1, which is the cheapest possible check that the enumeration is complete and the weights are right.

From that one pass the page gets the exact mean, standard deviation, median, 95th and 99th percentiles and upper-tail probability of every index at once. No sampling, no bootstrap and no asymptotics anywhere in the null.

2. The same expectation, two ways

The closed form for E[MI] is a triple sum over the hypergeometric support, and the enumeration is a sum over tables. They share no code and no loop structure. On the default world they agree to about 1.5e-15, and E[S] matches A·B/P to the last bit, which is the strongest available evidence that both are right. A single implementation of an expectation is a claim; two are a measurement.

3. Data with no seed in it

The dataset is deterministic on purpose. Class g has m members and they are placed at g·sep + σΦ-1((t+½)/m) for t = 0…m−1 — the quantiles of its own normal, not a draw from it. So there is no run-to-run variation in any headline, nothing to average over, and a reader who changes a slider sees the mechanism move rather than the noise. Where the page does sample — the random labellings, the Monte Carlo checks on the exact null — the column says so and carries a standard error.

4. k-means without Lloyd's algorithm

The argmax claim in section 5 of UNDERSTAND would be worthless if the clustering at each k were a local optimum, because then the metric might be preferring a lucky restart rather than a value of k. In one dimension the optimal k-means partition is contiguous in sorted order, so it is a dynamic program: with prefix sums of x and x² the cost of any segment is O(1), and kmeans1dAll builds every layer in one O(k n²) sweep, returning the exact optimum for all k from 1 to n together with a back-pointer table to recover the labels. The verifier checks the contiguity theorem the only honest way, by brute-forcing all set partitions of a small dataset and confirming the dynamic program found the global optimum among them and not merely among the contiguous ones.

5. Where the identities live

Six things on this page are algebraic identities rather than measurements, and each one is asserted with a tolerance chosen from what the arithmetic can deliver:

V-measure                  == 2 MI / (H(U) + H(V))      1e-15
NMI(min)                   == max(homogeneity, completeness)
NMI(max)                   == min(homogeneity, completeness)
homogeneity                == MI / H(U)
MI(refinement of U)        == H(U)                       exact for large pieces
AMI(min) on a refinement   == 1                          numerator IS denominator
E[ARI]                     == 0                          6e-17 over the enumeration

The refinement identity is the one worth watching: it is bit-exact while the pieces are large, and it drifts to about 5e-15 at pieces of one, because MI is then a sum of n terms each of which is a separate logarithm while H(U) is a sum of k. The page prints the residual rather than hiding it inside a tolerance, and the assertion is written at 1e-13 with the reason stated.

6. Two nulls, and the difference is not a bug

There are two things “a clustering that knows nothing” can mean. Fix the cluster sizes and permute which items go where — that is the fixed-margin null, it is what ARI and AMI correct for, and it is the one with the closed form. Or draw a label uniformly for each item, which is what you would actually do to simulate an ignorant clusterer — and that has random margins, and at k' = 100 on 240 items it leaves about nine clusters empty, so the realised k' is smaller and the realised score is lower. The page measures both against the exact number: the permutation draw lands on E[MI]/norm within a standard error, and the uniform draw sits visibly below it. Neither is wrong; they are different experiments, and conflating them is how a chance level gets quoted 12% too low.

7. Guards

Two samplers, two seed streams, one exact target. The permutation sampler is run with multiplicative-hash seeds and with the arithmetic stride that produced Day 69's artefact, both against the closed-form E[NMI], and both numbers are printed with the standard error beside them instead of being summarised as a verdict. This is the fourth consecutive re-measurement of that artefact and the third time it has failed to reproduce, which is now the more informative result.

The enumeration also has a cap. Margins with many small clusters blow up combinatorially — three classes over eighteen items split into nine pairs is 352,128 tables — so enumerateTables takes a ceiling and the table reports over cap rather than freezing the tab. Where the cap bites, the closed-form expectation is still exact and only the quantiles go missing, and the row says which.

8. Verification

The block between ENGINE:BEGIN and ENGINE:END is DOM-free, is extracted by regex and is run under Node against assertions written independently of it: xorshift32 in place of mulberry32; the multiple hypergeometric weights in exact BigInt rational arithmetic against the page's log-gammas, so the enumeration weights are shown to sum to exactly one and not merely to 1e-14 of it; E[MI] a third time by enumerating all n! relabellings of a tiny dataset, which is the definition the closed form and the table enumeration are both standing in for; the pair counts recomputed by an explicit O(n²) loop over all C(n,2) pairs instead of by ΣC(nij,2); mutual information recomputed as H(U)+H(V)−H(U,V) from the joint entropy rather than as a sum of p log(p/pq); lgamma against exact BigInt factorials; the inverse normal against a Simpson-integrated normal CDF; and the exact k-means optimum against a brute force over every set partition of a ten-point dataset, 115,975 of them, which tests the contiguity theorem the dynamic program is built on rather than the dynamic program alone.
